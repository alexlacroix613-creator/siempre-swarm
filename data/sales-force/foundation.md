## 2. Federal Compliance Framework

All state agents operate within a federal alcohol regulatory system designed to prevent vertical integration (tied-house rules), ensure price transparency, protect distributor territories, and prevent deceptive marketing.

### TTB Core Constraints

**Tied-House Prohibition (FAA Section 101)**
- Do not give retailers, distributors, or consumers things of value outside permitted exceptions
- Permitted exceptions: point-of-sale materials, limited branded merchandise, staff training, samples (with permits)
- Violations: unauthorized incentives, illegal subsidies, rebates outside pre-approved programs

**Sample Licenses and Controls**
- Limited direct-to-retailer sampling requires TTB permits
- All samples must comply with state-level sampling rules (often stricter than federal)
- Tasting events and on-premise samples require local approval and documentation

**Point-of-Sale Rules**
- POS materials (signs, tent cards, shelf talkers) must be factually accurate
- Claims must be substantiated (no false production stories, origin claims)
- Alcohol content, health, or political messaging restricted per state/federal law

**Price Transparency**
- Federal law does not set prices, but control states file pricing; open states allow negotiated pricing
- Pricing promotions and bill-backs must be documented, auditable, and within state law
- Unequal pricing to competing retailers can trigger trade-practice complaints

### State vs Federal Authority

- **Federal (TTB)**: Trade practice guardrails, label approval (COLA), import licensing, deceptive practice prevention
- **State**: Control of distribution route-to-market (who wholesales, what margins apply), sampling rules, listing cycles, price filing requirements, sales solicitation licensing
- **Local (City/County)**: On-premise licensing, event approval, local ordinances

### Control vs License vs Franchise Structure

**Control States (17 + control jurisdictions)**
- State government is the wholesale monopoly
- Supplier sells to state agency; state agency sells to retailers
- State controls pricing, listing, inventory, and timing
- Listing cycles and state-board approval are critical gatekeepers
- Examples: Alabama, Alaska (some), Idaho, Iowa, Maine, Michigan, Mississippi, Montana, New Hampshire, North Carolina, Ohio, Oregon, Pennsylvania, Utah, Vermont, Virginia, West Virginia, Wyoming

**License States (33 + mixed)**
- Private wholesalers/distributors are licensed by the state but operate independently
- Supplier sells direct to wholesaler; wholesaler sells to retailers
- Pricing is negotiated (not filed with state) unless state requires price posting
- Distributor relationships and performance are direct levers
- Key example: California, New York, Texas, Florida, Illinois, and most major metros

**Franchise States (flagged in matrix)**
- Hybrid approach: wholesaler protection laws exist; switching distributors requires state approval or compensation
- Typically license states with additional distributor tenure protections
- Examples: Some provisions in Ohio, Indiana, Tennessee

**Jurisdiction-Sensitive States**
- Mixed control/license structures within the state (e.g., Alaska, Maryland, Minnesota, South Dakota)
- Require state-by-state or even county-by-county verification before launch
- Extra caution on pricing and distributor strategy

## 7. State Manager Operating Framework

State managers own the market model: distributor strategy, account priorities, pricing discipline, program execution, depletions, relationships, and compliance. Success is measured by shipments, depletions, distribution, pricing integrity, and team adoption.

### Core Mandate (One Sentence)

> **Set the state plan. Get the distributor to execute it. Personally move the biggest accounts. Protect pricing, compliance, and the brand's long-term health.**

### Six-Bucket Scorecard Framework

| Bucket | Primary KPIs | Why It Matters |
|--------|--------------|-----------------|
| **Volume** | Shipments, depletions, 9L cases, revenue, gross margin | Tells you if the market is actually growing |
| **Distribution** | ACV/WDP, new placements, SKU count, menu/features | Shows whether brand is widening footprint |
| **Velocity** | ROS (rate of sale), depleted cases per point, menu turns, feature lift | Separates real wins from vanity placements |
| **Execution** | Display count, menu compliance, feature compliance, POS installed | Measures whether plans made it to store/bar level |
| **Pricing** | Shelf price, by-the-drink price, promo price, margin health | Prevents value destruction and channel conflict |
| **People** | Distributor focus, rep ride-withs, trainings, account call coverage | Great plans die without field adoption |

### 30/60/90 Day Ramp Structure

**Days 1–30: Learn the Market**
- Distributor walk-throughs (every major market, top 25 accounts)
- Understand distributor incentive structure, rep coverage, historical performance
- Build account universe (chains, independents, on-premise, off-premise)
- Review control-state listing status, pricing calendars, special-order processes
- Understand internal team (sales ops, finance, compliance, marketing)
- Establish weekly scorecard baseline and reporting cadence

**Days 31–60: Build the Plan**
- Develop 90-day state priorities: top 10 target accounts, channel mix, velocity targets
- Run first distributor business review with manager support
- Confirm pricing strategy and approve exceptions
- Launch compliance review: TTB tied-house, sample policy, POS rules, event rules
- Execute first field ride-withs and account calls (min. 2 ride-with days/week)
- Lock 30-day targets (depletions, new PODs, displays, price compliance)

**Days 61–90: Execute and Measure**
- Weekly scorecard tracking (11 KPIs + health flags)
- Bi-weekly account updates to key customers
- Run second distributor business review; escalate KPI misses
- Monthly forecast updates; adjust 90-day targets if needed
- Prepare quarterly business review deck (market snapshot, KPI trends, 90-day wins, 120-day plan)

### Operating Cadence

| Frequency | Activity | Owner |
|-----------|----------|-------|
| **Daily** | Check-in with distributor; field visits, account calls, compliance flags (including PBG FOB status) | State Manager |
| **Weekly** | Scorecard entry, ride-with days (min. 2), account updates | State Manager + Distributor |
| **Biweekly** | Pricing compliance review, trade-spend approvals, chain follow-ups | State Manager + Finance |
| **Monthly** | Distributor business review (KPIs, forecast, issues), account deep dives | State Manager + Distributor Leadership |
| **Quarterly** | Quarterly business review (deck, budget forecast, strategic account updates, forecast) | State Manager + Regional/National Leadership |

### Non-Negotiables (Hard Constraints)

1. **Compliance first:** No pricing, samples, POS, or promotions outside state/federal law
2. **Data discipline:** Weekly scorecard entry, no exceptions
3. **Distributor accountability:** Monthly business review with scorecard; misses require corrective action
4. **Account management:** Top 10 accounts get biweekly touch (call, visit, or written update)
5. **Field truth:** Minimum 2 ride-with days per week; shelf/menu/display verification
6. **Pricing integrity:** Track shelf price, by-the-drink price, and promo pricing weekly; escalate variance >3%
7. **National program translation:** Confirm national account programs land locally; escalate slippage same week

## 11. Escalation Matrix

State agents escalate to Compliance, Legal, or Finance when these conditions are triggered.

### When to Escalate to Compliance

- Any new pricing structure not pre-approved in the state pricing calendar
- Any non-standard sample request (outside permitted sample license scope)
- Any point-of-sale material not reviewed by compliance (claims, health statements, origin stories)
- Any tied-house concern: free merchandise, unauthorized incentives, or retail subsidies
- Any distributor change or pricing disruption in a franchise-flagged state
- Any state-listed change (delisting risk, relisting, or inventory management question)
- Tasting events, promotional activations, or field staff licensing requirements
- Monthly audit: price compliance review (spot-check 10% of top accounts for shelf/BTD price alignment)

### When to Escalate to Legal

- Distributor agreement termination or non-renewal
- Merger, acquisition, or distributor consolidation in your state
- Tied-house complaint or trade-practice inquiry
- Pricing discrimination complaint (competitive retailer claiming unequal treatment)
- Brand registration or label change (state-specific COLA or filing requirement)
- Chain or chain-owned distributor conflict of interest
- Distributor non-performance claim or litigation threat

### When to Escalate to Finance

- Trade spend overages >10% of monthly budget
- Bill-back or rebate request outside pre-approved programs
- Pricing exception request (below cost, above ceiling, or conditional discount)
- Margin compression >5% vs plan
- Distributor margin change or incentive restructure
- Forecast variance >20% for two consecutive weeks

### When to Escalate to Sales Leadership

- Market underperformance >20% vs annual plan (depletions, distribution, or velocity)
- Distributor performance critical (KPI misses, leadership change, or capability gap)
- Competitive threat (major account loss, pricing war, or category disruption)
- National account program failure (HQ win not translating to local execution)
- New market entry or major account opportunity requiring strategic decision
- Quarterly business review prep (forecast, plan adjustment, resource request)
