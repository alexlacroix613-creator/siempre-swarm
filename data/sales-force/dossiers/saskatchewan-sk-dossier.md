## 2. REGULATORY FRAMEWORK

### SLGA (Saskatchewan Liquor and Gaming Authority)
- **Primary Regulator:** Provincial monopoly body controlling spirits licensing and wholesale pricing
- **Wholesale System:** SLGA sets mandatory wholesale markup structure for all suppliers
- **Small Producer Classification:** Recent change providing reduced markup due to production volume threshold
- **Product Registration:** All SKUs must be registered with SLGA with valid SCC codes

### Small Producer Status (NEW - EFFECTIVE MARCH 25, 2026)

**Qualification Date:** March 25, 2026
**Effective Date:** Immediate (already live in SLGA system)

**Impact on Pricing:**
- **Plata:** Wholesale reduced from $380/case to $300/case (markup reduction ~21%)
- **Retail Target:** Sub-$70 (previously ~$84)
- **Reposado:** Similar markup reduction applied
- **Añejo:** Eligible for immediate margin correction (was 10%, now optimizable)

**Why Small Producer?**
- Recognizes higher per-unit costs for smaller production volumes
- Designed to help craft/small producers compete with large brands
- Margin philosophy: NOT to be lowest-shelf option, but competitive with larger producers

**Key Implication:**
- This is a **permanent structural change**, not temporary
- Significant near-term pricing opportunity to capture market share
- Enables margin cleanup on under-priced SKUs (Añejo at 10% margin)

### Licensing & Approval Workflow
1. Supplier qualification approval (completed March 25, 2026)
2. SCC code registration with SLGA (required for product hold-release)
3. Wholesale pricing submission through SLGA system
4. Depletion reporting via SLGA ELLIS portal

### Key Process Notes
- **SLGA Communication:** Suppliers do NOT receive direct notification from SLGA when pricing changes take effect; information flows through distributor/agent first
- **Transparency Gap:** Monica Sanita flagged frustration that SLGA doesn't notify suppliers directly when they qualify for programs (small producer declaration was submitted, but approval came via BevCo)
